### 1.2 Probe Verdict on the Riskiest Assumption
- **Sample long-form outputs committed in-repo:** **2 of 5** (`assafelovic/gpt-researcher`, `dzhng/deep-research`).

## 2. Known Tradeoffs with Measured Evidence (Q3)

Across both external benchmarks and internal repository experiments, **seven major tradeoffs** govern AI research methods. Every tradeoff below has direct quantitative evidence from fetched sources.

### Tradeoff 1: Token / Compute Spend vs. Coverage & Quality (and Unbounded Cost Inflation)
- **Evidence that tokens buy retrieval & coverage:**
  - **Anthropic (`BrowseComp` variance decomposition):** Across browsing agent runs on BrowseComp, three factors explained **95% of performance variance**, and **token usage alone explained 80% of variance** (with tool-call count and model choice accounting for the remaining 15%). Upgrading the base model (`Claude Sonnet 4` vs `Claude Sonnet 3.7`) provided a larger efficiency multiplier than doubling the token budget on the weaker model ([Anthropic Multi-Agent Research System](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **OpenAI (`BrowseComp` test-time compute scaling):** Accuracy of OpenAI Deep Research on 1,266 BrowseComp problems scaled smoothly with log test-time compute, jumping from **0.6%** (`GPT-4o`), **1.9%** (`GPT-4o w/ browsing`), and **9.9%** (`OpenAI o1`) to **51.5%** (`OpenAI Deep Research`) ([BrowseComp, Wei et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12516v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **GPT-Researcher (`context_filter` replay benchmark, $n=8$ open-ended tasks):** Passing pages unfiltered (`none`: median **61.3k chars** context, **$0.284/report**) beat embedding-filtered context (`embeddings`: **8.8k chars**, **$0.155/report**) **8 wins to 0 losses** in blind pairwise judging, and beat Jev-filtered context (`jev`: **9.1k chars**, **$0.160/report**) **6 wins, 2 ties, 0 losses** — at **65–83% higher dollar cost** and risk of overflowing context windows on deep runs ([GPT-Researcher Context Filter Benchmark](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/evals/context_filter/README.md) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **Internal Hybrid Comparison (`[repo: research-pipeline-hybrid-comparison/README.md]`):** Method C (Hybrid: **~548k tokens**, **180 tool calls**, **~14 min** wall time) retrieved **88 unique URLs across 36 domains** and won pairwise usefulness **2/2** against Method B (Deep-Research Skill: **~382k tokens**, **87 tool calls**, **~5.5 min**, **40 URLs across 22 domains**) and Method A (Repo Default: **~348k + ~25k tokens**, **153 tool calls**, **~5 min**, **57 URLs across 24 domains**).
- **Evidence of diminishing returns & wasted tokens when undirected:**
  - **Princeton (`AI Agents That Matter`, Kapoor et al.):** Complex agent architectures on HumanEval and HotPotQA varied in cost by **50× to 100×** (reaching >$100 per evaluation run) while failing to beat simple zero-shot or 5-retry baselines ([AI Agents That Matter, Kapoor et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01502v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **GPT-Researcher (`SimpleQA` saturation, $n=20$):** On factual lookup questions, unfiltered context (`$0.192/report`) and Jev/BM25/embeddings (`$0.107–$0.117/report`) all scored **18–20 out of 20** (Jev vs `none` was 6W–10T–4L) — extra context tokens added zero factual accuracy once search found the fact ([GPT-Researcher Context Filter Benchmark](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/evals/context_filter/README.md) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **Internal Web-Search A/B (`[repo: web-search-methodology/README.md]`):** Directed R3 methodology (**26,213 tokens**, 3–5 targeted fetches + cross-domain verification) matched Naive R2 quality (**93.5%**) at **53% of the token cost** (vs **49,624 tokens**), achieving **2.75 vs 1.45 quality points per 1k tokens** (1.9× efficiency).

### Tradeoff 2: Citation Count (Breadth) vs. Citation Faithfulness ("Citation Theater" & Post-Rationalization)
- **Measured inverse correlation across commercial DR agents:**
  - **DeepResearch Bench I (`FACT` evaluation across 100 tasks, Table 1):** `Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research` generated the highest number of effective citations (**111.21 per report**) and highest comprehensiveness (**48.53**), but its Citation Accuracy was **81.44%**, and `OpenAI Deep Research` averaged **40.79 citations** at **77.96% accuracy**. Conversely, `Perplexity Deep Research` cited only **31.26 sources** at **90.24% accuracy**, and `Claude-3.5-Sonnet w/Search` cited **9.78 sources** at **94.04% accuracy** ([DeepResearch Bench, Du et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11763v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **ResearchRubrics (101 tasks, Section 4.2):** Confirmed the exact same tradeoff: `Gemini DR` averaged **111 citations with 81% accuracy**, whereas `Perplexity DR` averaged **31 citations with 90% accuracy**. As the authors conclude: *"Systems optimized for comprehensive coverage sacrifice precision, while those targeting accuracy miss crucial perspectives"* ([ResearchRubrics, Sharma et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07685v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **DeepScholar-Bench (academic literature synthesis, Table 2):** `OpenAI DeepResearch` achieved the highest Reference Coverage (**0.187**) and Nugget Coverage (**0.392**), **but its Citation Precision dropped to 0.399 and Claim Coverage to 0.138** — whereas `DeepScholar-ref (GPT-4.1, Claude)` achieved **0.944 Citation Precision** and **0.895 Claim Coverage** with lower Reference Coverage (**0.152**) ([DeepScholar-Bench, Patel et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20033v2) — accessed 2026-11-09).
- **"Citation Theater" (decorative references vs claim support vs post-rationalization):**
  - **GPT-Researcher (`evals/README.md`):** Explicitly separates **Citation Faithfulness** (whether URLs listed in `## References` are cited in the body — scoring **0.94** across 5 sample runs) from **Unsupported Claim Rate** (whether the cited source text actually supports the claim — where the same sample runs had an **average unsupported claim rate of 0.535** and average claim score of **0.428**!) ([GPT-Researcher Evals README](https://github.com/assafelovic/gpt-researcher/blob/main/evals/README.md) — accessed 2026-11-09). A report can have 94% well-formatted inline footnotes while 53.5% of its claims are unsupported by the scraped text.
  - **Post-rationalized citations (`Wallat et al. 2024`):** Even when a cited document entails the claim (*citation correctness*), counterfactual injection experiments show that **up to 57% of RAG citations are post-rationalized** (the model generated the claim from parametric prior beliefs and attached a plausible-looking retrieved document without causally relying on it, citing forged relevant-uncited docs **57%** of the time and docs cited for other reasons **55%** of the time) ([Wallat et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.18004v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).

### Tradeoff 3: Report Length & Surface Polish vs. Human Usability & Substantive Accuracy
- **Length-quality conflation in rubric/LLM judges vs human preference:**
  - **AlpacaEval Length-Controlled (`Dubois et al. 2024`):** Standard LLM-as-a-judge win rates for `gpt4_1106_preview` fluctuated from **22.9% (concise prompt) to 64.3% (verbose prompt)** purely by varying output length instructions. Controlling for length via generalized linear model regression increased Spearman correlation with human LMSYS Chatbot Arena rankings from **0.94 to 0.98** ([Length-Controlled AlpacaEval, Dubois et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04475v2) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **ResearchRubrics (Section 4.2 & Table 9):** Output length varied 4× across systems (`Gemini DR` mean **7,519 words / 12,153 tokens**, `ChatGPT DR` **6,270 words / 10,170 tokens**, `Perplexity DR` **1,829 words / 3,664 tokens**). Response length correlated positively with criterion compliance ($r \approx 0.24\text{--}0.28$), because longer reports hit more checklist items even when padded ([ResearchRubrics, Sharma et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07685v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **Mind2Web 2 (Table 3):** On long-horizon tasks where human annotators achieved **54% full-task success** in **186 ± 27 words**, `Gemini Deep Research` wrote **3,357 ± 49 words (18× longer)** and achieved only **18% success**, whereas `OpenAI Deep Research` wrote **559 ± 19 words (6× shorter than Gemini)** and achieved **28% success** ([Mind2Web 2, Gou et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21506v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **DeepResearch Bench II (Table 3):** Every tested DRA scored **74.59%–91.85% on Presentation** (e.g., `Grok Deep Search` **91.42%**, `Tongyi Deep Research` **86.13%**) while scoring only **22.95%–39.98% on Information Recall** ([DeepResearch Bench II, Li et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08536v3) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **Internal Hybrid Comparison (`[repo: research-pipeline-hybrid-comparison/README.md]`):** Method C's 7,739-word README scored **5/5 on coverage and groundedness** but **2/5 on readability and actionability** in the 3-way blind judge (which preferred Method B's concise 3,231-word single report **39 to 30**), because C's length and proposal Q-numbering read "like a lab report, not an answer."

### Tradeoff 4: Multi-Agent Fan-Out & Separate Writer vs. Token Cost & Synthesis Claim Drift
- **Cost vs parallel breadth:** Anthropic's multi-agent system (`Claude Opus 4` lead + `Claude Sonnet 4` parallel subagents) beat single-agent `Claude Opus 4` by **90.2%** on internal breadth-first research evals and cut research time by up to 90%, **but consumed ~15× more tokens than standard chat interactions (and ~3.75× more than single agents, which used ~4× chat tokens)** ([Anthropic Multi-Agent Research System](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) — accessed 2026-11-09).
- **Separate writer isolation vs claim drift (`[repo: research-pipeline-hybrid-comparison/README.md]`):**
  - Isolating parallel researchers from a separate writer agent (Arms B and C) kept the main context window clean and produced the most actionable package (C won pairwise **2/2** with `ai-instructions.md`).
  - **However, handing researcher notes to a separate writer introduced claim drift:** Arm A (where the same session that coordinated research wrote the synthesis) had **94% (15/16) fully supported claims (0 unsupported)**, whereas Arm C (separate repo writer) had **81% (26/32) fully supported (5 partly supported, 1 unsupported)** and Arm B (separate skill writer) had **74% (26/35) fully supported (8 partly supported, 1 unsupported)** — including fabricated quote rewordings inside quotation marks and scope-stretching of benchmark numbers (`[repo: research-pipeline-hybrid-comparison/README.md]`).

### Tradeoff 5: Web Browsing Access vs. Confidence Calibration Error
- **Evidence (`BrowseComp`, Table 3):** Giving an LLM web browsing tools dramatically increases task accuracy on hard queries (`OpenAI Deep Research` **51.5%** vs `o1` **9.9%** and `GPT-4o` **0.6%**), **but significantly worsens calibration error**: `GPT-4o` without browsing had **69%** calibration error vs **82%** *with* browsing, and `OpenAI Deep Research` reached **91% calibration error** ([BrowseComp, Wei et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12516v1) — accessed 2026-11-09). Finding partial or tangential web pages makes browsing agents highly confident in wrong answers.
- **Mitigation via explicit abstention (`PaperQA2`):** Allowing an agent to output `"insufficient information"` (which PaperQA2 chose on **21.9% ± 0.9%** of LitQA2 questions) enabled **85.2% ± 1.1% precision** (beating human PhDs at **73.8% ± 9.6%**) while keeping overall accuracy at **66.0% ± 1.2%** ([PaperQA2, Skarlinski et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13740v2) — accessed 2026-11-09).

### Tradeoff 6: Cross-Study Note Reuse vs. Error Propagation
- **Evidence (`[repo: research-duplication-and-reuse/README.md]`):** Instructing a 30-turn researcher agent to read prior repo notes before searching the web saved only **3% of tokens** (830k vs 858k mean across 3 tasks; target was 25%) because the agent still spent its remaining turn budget on the web and carried the long repo notes in context across every subsequent call. Worse, on the immersion task (`T1`), the reuse run **copied an earlier repo error (`Spearman rho = .724` misattributed to the full 31-item IEQ) into one of its 5 headline claims**, even after re-fetching the original paper (`[repo: research-duplication-and-reuse/README.md]`; [Xiong et al. 2025 (arXiv:2505.16067v1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).

### Tradeoff 7: Single-Run Evaluation Cost vs. Run-to-Run Variance (Rival Hypothesis B)
- **Evidence across benchmarks and internal runs:**
  - **$\tau$-bench (`Yao et al. 2024`):** `gpt-4o` dropped from **~61% single-run success ($\text{pass}^1$)** to **<25% 8-run consistency ($\text{pass}^8$)** ([$\tau$-bench, Yao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12045v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **Mind2Web 2 (`Gou et al. 2025`, Table 3):** `OpenAI Deep Research` scored **28% single-run Success Rate** vs **40% Pass@3** (a 12-point spread across 3 runs on the same tasks) ([Mind2Web 2, Gou et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21506v1) — accessed 2026-11-09).
  - **Internal Q5 baseline pairs (`[repo: research-duplication-and-reuse/README.md]`):** Two identical baseline runs (`base-r1` vs `base-r2`) on the exact same question (`T1`) used **855k vs 1,334k tokens** (a 1.56× spread), found **9 vs 15 new sources**, and had **13% vs 9% fact-check change rates** (on `T2`: **594k vs 1,010k tokens** and **4% vs 12% change rates**).
  - **Internal Judge Position Bias (`[repo: research-pipeline-hybrid-comparison/README.md]`):** Arm A's blind judge score moved **7 points (30/40 vs 37/40)** simply by reversing the reading order between Run 1 and Run 2.
  - **Implication for Rival Hypothesis B:** Run-to-run variance on tokens ($\pm 35\%$), source count ($\pm 25\%$), and claim error rate ($\pm 4\text{--}8\text{ pp}$) is larger than small (<15%) single-run differences between methods, confirming that single-run differences under ~15–20% cannot distinguish methods without $k \ge 3$ trials.

## 3. External Measurability Tiers & Gaming Analysis (Q4)

Because the 5-repo probe showed that most public repositories do not publish committed sample reports (only 2/5) or cost logs (only 1/5), every metric must be classified by **what access level is required to score it from outside** and **how easily it can be gamed**.

| Tier | Access Required | What Can Be Measured | Gaming Vulnerability & Anti-Gaming Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier A: Desk-Measurable** (100% of repos) | Read-only inspection of repository code, prompts, schemas, and commit tree (`gh api`). Costs $0 and ~5–10 min/repo. | **Architecture & process controls:**<br>1. Decomposition / planning step before search.<br>2. Context / subagent isolation (prevents raw-scrape context bloat).<br>3. Explicit search/turn/token budget caps.<br>4. Post-draft verification / faithfulness / NLI check step.<br>5. Abstention / uncertainty / `(unverified)` handling in prompts.<br>6. Treat GitHub stars and self-reported leaderboard PNGs without committed logs as **weak evidence**. |

## 4. What Fits Our Work & Existing Repo Baseline Values (Q5)

### 4.1 Why No Single Published Benchmark Disconfirms the Prior
- **Proposal Disconfirmer:** *"A published benchmark for long-form research that at least 3 independent projects use and that has been shown to agree with human ratings. If that exists, we adopt it instead of building a scorecard."*
- **Finding:** **Disconfirmer did NOT fire.**
  1. While four strong long-form benchmarks launched in 2025–2026 (`DeepResearch Bench I/II`, `ResearchRubrics`, `DeepScholar-Bench`, `Mind2Web 2`) and all four report human-judge validation (71.33% PAR on DRB I; 91.75% ACC / 89.57% F1 on DRB II; 0.72–0.76 Macro F1 on ResearchRubrics; 95.14%–98.89% node agreement on Mind2Web 2), **none is adopted across $\ge 3$ independent open-source research-method repos** (in our 5-repo probe, 0 of 5 ran any of these four long-form benchmarks in their repos; `gpt-researcher` used `SimpleQA` + custom 6-metric quality eval, `storm` used `FreshWiki`, and `Alibaba-NLP/DeepResearch` used short-answer `BrowseComp`/`HLE`/`DeepSearch`).
  2. Furthermore, all four benchmarks evaluate **single-artifact outputs** (`Report Quality` + `Citation Support`). **None of the four scores method-level token cost, human effort, cross-study reuse, desk-level architecture, or multi-run consistency ($\text{pass}^k$)** (only `Mind2Web 2` records wall-clock minutes and `Pass@3`).
  3. Therefore, we adopt the **best validated building blocks** from those benchmarks (binary rubric checklists from `ResearchRubrics`/`DRB II`, claim-level URL support from `FACT`/`ALCE`/`Mind2Web 2`, length control from `AlpacaEval-LC`, and Pareto cost/variance tracking from `Kapoor et al.` and `$\tau$-bench`) into our two-part `SCORECARD.md`.
