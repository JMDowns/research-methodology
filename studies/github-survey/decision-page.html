Written Methods Beat Popular Apps on Verification; Borrow Deterministic Gates

Data & Scope: Survey of 72 Screened and 24 In-Depth Scored Public GitHub AI Research Methods vs. ~/dev/research/ across Studies 00-how-to-measure-a-research-method and 01-github-research-methods-survey (2026-11-09)

Our research method ties for the highest architectural desk score across the field, and no external GitHub method beats our independently measured claim faithfulness at lower cost, so we should borrow deterministic verification scripts from written-method peers rather than switching frameworks.

What these studies asked: Study 00 asked how to measure a whole research method fairly across output quality, token/time cost, repeatability, and desk-inspectable architecture when public repos rarely publish outputs or costs. Study 01 applied that 3-tier scorecard (SCORECARD.md) to 72 public GitHub repositories across four buckets: Type A (research apps and frameworks like gpt-researcher), Type B (written methods, skills, and persistent LLM wikis like hyperresearch and ours), Type C (scientific and literature agents like STORM and PaperQA2), and Type D (benchmark-driven RL agents like Alibaba-NLP/DeepResearch).

What the verdict depends on & earlier conclusions: This verdict depends on whether long-form decision studies require verified claim-to-source entailment (O3: fraction of sampled factual claims supported by their cited URL) and explicit uncertainty handling (D5: marking (unverified) or Known Gaps instead of guessing). It confirms and extends our earlier 2×2 study (research-pipeline-hybrid-comparison/): written multi-agent workflows with surgical post-draft patching beat monolithic report-rewriting apps on faithfulness while avoiding 30B local GPU lock-in.
